URINARY SYSTEM & RENAL PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
A five-part study reference

Overview

key facts only

The rules, thresholds and definitions worth memorizing — no long lecture text.

Kidney function

Main job

Make urine → control fluid & electrolyte balance, eliminate waste.

Filtration

Kidneys filter blood, then reabsorb and secrete ions.

Electrolytes controlled

Na, K, Cl, CO2 and body fluid balance.

Urinary tract infection (UTI)

Etiology

Bacterial infection into the urinary tract.

Signs & symptoms

Frequency, dysuria, hematuria, turbid urine, unusual odor, fever.

Renal failure — the numbers

Definition

Loss of renal function in both kidneys until only 10–20% of normal GFR remains.

Acute

Abrupt onset — potentially reversible.

Chronic

Progresses over ≥ 3 months — can become permanent.

Stages of chronic renal failure (by GFR)

Diminished renal reserve

GFR 35–50% of normal.

Renal insufficiency

GFR 20–35% of normal.

Renal failure

GFR < 20% of normal.

End-stage renal disease

GFR < 5% of normal.

Glomerular vs. tubular dysfunction

Glomerular dysfunction

↓ GFR, retention of filtered substances incl. water — e.g. glomerulonephritis.

Tubular dysfunction

Polyuria of dilute urine, low specific gravity, loss of electrolytes/nutrients — e.g. aminoglycoside nephrotoxicity.

Consequences of falling GFR

↓ Erythropoietin

→ Anemia.

↓ Vitamin D

→ Hypocalcemia, secondary hyperparathyroidism, hyperphosphatemia.

↓ Excretion

→ Acidosis, hyperkalemia, hypertension, edema.

Platelet dysfunction

→ Increased bleeding tendency.

Nephritic syndrome — the rule

Core definition

Hematuria + at least one of: oliguria (<400 mL/24h), hypertension with mild-moderate proteinuria, uremic symptoms, edema, elevated BUN.

Mechanism

Inflammation + thinning of GBM, pores form in podocytes → protein and RBCs leak into urine.

Nephrotic syndrome — the rule

Threshold

Proteinuria ≥ 3.5 g/day in adults.

Full picture

Proteinuria + lipiduria + hypoalbuminemia + generalized edema + hyperlipidemia.

Primary causes

Minimal change disease (most common — children), membranous nephropathy (most common — adults), FSGS, membranoproliferative GN.

Secondary causes

DM, amyloidosis, infections (Hep B/C, HIV, syphilis), malignancy, drugs (NSAIDs, gold, penicillamine, heavy metals).

Metabolic consequences of nephrotic syndrome

Infection risk

Loss of complement + immunoglobulin.

Hyperlipidemia

↑ Hepatic LDL/VLDL synthesis → atherosclerosis.

Hypercoagulability

Loss of antithrombin III.

Also seen

Hypocalcemia, hypovolemia.

Specific diseases — one line each

Diabetic nephropathy

Chronic albuminuria ≥3–6 months on 2 of 3 urine collections + DM history + retinopathy + HTN + ↓GFR.

Henoch–Schönlein purpura

Systemic form of IgA nephropathy; children; palpable purpura, abdominal pain, arthritis; IgA deposits in glomerulus.

Systemic lupus erythematosus

Autoantibody immune complexes deposit along GBM → glomerular inflammation → nephrotic syndrome.

Chronic glomerulonephritis

Irreversible, progressive glomerular + tubulointerstitial fibrosis → CKD → ESRD.

Polycystic kidney disease

Genetic; enlarged cystic kidneys, hypertension, UTI, dilute urine, pain, hematuria.

Interstitial nephritis

Affects tubules/interstitium — does not cause nephrotic syndrome (glomeruli spared).
